# Panorama

> The stereo field as a picture — horizontal is where a sound sits between your speakers, vertical is its pitch. A centred kick anchors the bottom while hats and reverb fan to the edges; a mono mix collapses to one column. Best on well-produced stereo material.

- type: particle
- pace param: drift (lo=0.15 default=0.45 hi=0.9)
- motion (loud half, mean Δ): lo=0.0061 default=0.00632 hi=0.00655
- luma (quiet/loud at default): 0.0349 / 0.0734
- headless caveats: none

## Imagery
An oscilloscope-dark instrument view: a single razor-thin blue-white beam
runs top to bottom of a black frame, with faint vertical bands ghosting at
the left and right edges. Around the beam's lower half hangs a horizontal
carpet of speckle — on the loud half a broad icy-blue pointillist cloud,
brightest in a white-violet hotspot at bottom center (the kick), thinning
as it fans outward; higher up, a small dim olive-green speckle band (the
hats). The quiet half shows only the beam over a faint warm rust-colored
dust shelf.

## Motion character
Quiet instrument, not a mover: loud-half motion ~0.0063 mean reads as
shimmer — speckle twinkling and drifting sideways within fixed lanes while
the beam stands still. The drift sweep is nearly invisible in the numbers
(0.0061 / 0.0063 / 0.0066) and the lo/hi stills match; treat drift as a
texture nuance, not a pace control.

## Energy response
Brightness doubles quiet-to-loud (luma 0.035 → 0.073) and motion doubles
(0.0029 → 0.0063): the mono pad collapses to the lone center column over a
dim warm dust floor, then the full mix fans out into the wide cold-blue
speckle field with a hot kick anchor — the palette flips warm → icy.

## Palette
Black field; electric blue-white beam; ice-blue speckle with a violet
hotspot; olive band; rust dust when quiet. Very dark (luma ≤0.073),
high local contrast, cool-dominant.

## Casting notes
Casts as a scientific instrument: spectrogram-city at night, sonar, a
seismograph, rain lit by a single shaft of light — good for minimal, techy,
analytical moods and breakdowns. Very dark and sparse: cannot fill a frame
or carry a big drop alone, and the fixed center beam is always present.
Works as a crisp overlay over a soft bed (Lumen), or solo for stark
minimalism. The test track is near-mono; real stereo mixes should fan wider.
