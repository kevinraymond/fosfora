# Polycephalum

> Twelve slime-mold organisms — one per pitch class — flood the screen with veined pulsing networks and fight for territory. Loud pitch classes dominate; semitone neighbours repel while fifths cooperate, so a key change makes one species visibly conquer another's ground. The first effect keyed to the full chroma vector

- type: feedback
- pace param: turn_speed (lo=0.15 default=0.5 hi=0.9)
- motion (loud half, mean Δ): lo=0.0059 default=0.0060 hi=0.0059
- luma (quiet/loud at default): 0.011 / 0.033
- headless caveats: none

## Imagery
Hair-thin colored filaments — red, blue, violet, green threads — tracing fingerprint
whorls and marbled contour lines across a solid black field. Looks like a long-exposure
of iron filings, or a topographic map drawn in neon ink at 10% brightness. Mostly black:
the lines are the only content.

## Motion character
A slow crawl; measured motion is tiny (0.006 loud). turn_speed does not change speed at
all in the numbers (0.0059 across the sweep) — it changes line shape: lo draws long, open
topographic contours with wide black gaps; hi draws a tight, kinked labyrinth of short
scribbles. Reads as texture density, not pace.

## Energy response
The loud half triples both brightness (0.011 → 0.033) and motion (0.0021 → 0.0060): the
quiet half is a few dim red-blue threads, the loud half a dense multicolor swirl-field.
Documented response: chroma feeds per-species dominance, kicks burst the deposit, beats
pulse the trail decay.

## Palette
Black-dominant with thin saturated rainbow threads; extremely dark overall (luma 0.03 at
its brightest). The color lives entirely in the filaments.

## Casting notes
Near-black at default — flag: it cannot carry a frame alone at these levels. Cast it as an
underlay or texture layer: veins, roots, neural networks, mycelium, circuitry-organic
moods. Its signature trick (key changes making one color conquer another's territory)
needs tonal music and patience. Pairs under any bright foreground; useless as a solo
full-frame source without a boost.
