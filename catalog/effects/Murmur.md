# Murmur

> Starling murmuration — topological K=7 nearest neighbors, Vicsek noise order-chaos phase transitions, predator avoidance, dark bird silhouettes against twilight sky

- type: particle
- pace param: speed (lo=0.15 default=0.5 hi=0.9)
- motion (loud half, mean Δ): lo=0.00733 default=0.00826 hi=0.00892
- luma (quiet/loud at default): 0.3379 / 0.3345
- headless caveats: none

## Imagery
The only landscape of this batch: a full-frame twilight sky gradient — warm
amber-salmon above melting into slate blue-lavender below — with a dense
flock of thousands of tiny dark bird flecks massed mid-frame. The flock
reads as one soft-edged organism: a dome, a lens, a stretched cloud,
its core glinting white where birds bunch and catch rim light, its fringes
sparkling with faint green/magenta speckle. Stragglers trail off in thin
wisps at the edges.

## Motion character
Constant, even, unhurried drift — the flock is always moving (~0.008–0.010
mean Δ in both halves) but never jumps; motion reads as displacement of one
soft mass plus internal grain-swirl, no flicker. The speed sweep is subtle
on camera: lo 0.0073 → hi 0.0089 loud-half mean, about a 20% change — hi
reads as a slightly more scattered, ragged flock, lo as a tighter clump.

## Energy response
Nearly energy-flat by the numbers: luma 0.338 quiet vs 0.334 loud, motion
0.0096 vs 0.0083. The sky and flock persist regardless of the track; the
music re-shapes the flock (splits, density, silhouette crispness) rather
than brightening the frame. Do not cast it expecting a visible drop.

## Palette
Muted naturalistic dusk: apricot/amber top, dusty periwinkle-blue bottom,
charcoal-grey flock with white glints. Mid-high overall brightness for this
set, low saturation, gentle value range (luma_std ~0.06).

## Casting notes
Casts as nature footage: starlings at dusk, a swarm, wind-blown ash, a
breathing crowd. The one effect here with a bright full-frame sky — it
cannot do darkness, neon, or hard beat sync, and its loud/quiet halves look
nearly identical, so use it for serene, contemplative, or interstitial
passages rather than drops. As a base layer its pale gradient will dominate
any additive layer above it; better solo or with dark overlays.
