# Accretion

> Gravitational N-body simulation — audio injects massive seed particles that attract swarms, forming accretion discs, orbital systems, and slingshot ejections

- type: feedback
- pace param: none
- motion (loud half, mean Δ): default=0.0045
- luma (quiet/loud at default): 0.023 / 0.025
- headless caveats: none

## Imagery
A single dim particle cloud centre-frame on black. Quiet half: a furry rust-brown oval — thousands of fine dust filaments radiating from a dark hollow core, like a smoke ring seen face-on. Loud half: the cloud tightens into a faint spiral-galaxy swirl of tiny blue-grey star specks with a handful of small white comet streaks curving through it. The outer two-thirds of the frame is empty black.

## Motion character
Near-static by catalog scale (motion 0.003–0.005 in both halves; 0.03 is "moderate"). What movement exists is a slow rotational drift of the dust swirl with occasional individual streaks — churn at astronomical patience, no displacement of the mass itself. No pace sweep was rendered.

## Energy response
Almost none visible in the numbers: luma 0.023 → 0.025, motion 0.0034 → 0.0045. The visible change is structural, not energetic — the quiet fuzzy dust-ring reorganises into a spiral with comet streaks in the loud half. Kick and bass do not read as flashes or pulses in these renders.

## Palette
Rust brown and ember orange at the fringe, cold blue-grey speckle in the core, white streak highlights. Everything sits just above black (luma_std 0.028–0.036) — very low value, low saturation.

## Casting notes
Flag: renders are near-black and nearly static — this cannot carry a scene or read from a distance at these settings. Casts only as a subtle underlay: deep-space dust, a dying ember cloud, a slow galaxy behind brighter foreground layers. The shipped promise of discs and slingshots is only legible on close inspection of the stills; do not cast it for spectacle.
